How to read this page
The model behind these picks has shown no skill over last year's averages in Week 1 in three seasons of testing. These are paper picks by the engine's own rules: logged before kickoff, scored Monday against results and closing lines. As of 2026-09-03 the engine covers every skill position and market the book lists: passing yards and touchdowns, rushing yards, receiving yards and receptions for backs and tight ends as well as receivers, and same-game combinations priced from one simulation of the offense.

Three views. Picks is the sheet: the legs worth a bet, each with its price, the chance the price implies and the chance the model gives it. Games has one card per game in DraftKings order: kickoff, venue, roof, total, spread, the kickoff forecast, the verdict, the pick and its checks, the book's main lines and the players the model covers. All odds is every ladder the book listed, every rung, beside the model's chance.

Verdicts. Play: the model beats the price by a clear margin and the player's own record supports it. Lean: the model is ahead but the case has a hole. Pass: the book is at or above the model.

Parlays. Legs in different games multiply exactly, so the slip price is the product of the leg prices. For legs in the same game DraftKings reprices for correlation, and the displayed price will be lower than the product shown.

Weather. Each card shows the roof and the kickoff-hour forecast: weather.gov when its hourly forecast reaches the kickoff (about 6.5 days out), Open-Meteo before that, each labelled with the source. Rain enters the model as a chance shared by everyone in the game; wind of 15 mph or more shortens the passing game; indoor games carry no weather term.

Players tables. QB is the passer expected to start ("new" marks a change from last season, which the model discounts). Role is the share of the team's pass plays the receiver ran a route on last season, plus expected carries for backs; it is the volume input the model uses. Snap percent is for reference.

How the picks are made

Each game is simulated thousands of times as one offense: the play count, the split between dropbacks and runs, every receiver's targets, catches and yards, every back's carries and yards, and the quarterback's passing line as the sum of what his receivers do plus the throws to everyone else. A quarterback's passing yards and his receiver's yards are therefore priced together, and a back's carries fall in the same simulated games where the passing volume rises.

Behind that sit every receiver's route share, target rate, depth of target, catch rate and yards per catch, both teams' tempo, the total and spread, venue, kickoff window and forecast. The opposing defense enters as five measured terms (yards per target, catch rate, passing touchdowns per attempt, yards per carry and rushing touchdowns per carry allowed, each relative to the league and shrunk hard because defense barely repeats year to year) plus the total and spread. Teammates share the game's pass volume, so paired legs are priced together. A leg makes the list only when the model's number beats the price by a clear margin, the player's own record supports it, and a plain-language reason survives. Games a teammate missed count at 30%, games under a different quarterback at 50%, deep rungs are excluded, and players on a new team are shown but never picked.
