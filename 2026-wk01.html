How the picks are made

Each game is simulated thousands of times as one offense: the play count, the split between dropbacks and runs, every receiver's targets, catches and yards, every back's carries and yards, and the quarterback's passing line as the sum of what his receivers do plus the throws to everyone else. A quarterback's passing yards and his receiver's yards are therefore priced together, and a back's carries fall in the same simulated games where the passing volume rises.

Behind that sit every receiver's route share, target rate, depth of target, catch rate and yards per catch, both teams' tempo, the total and spread, the opponent's pass defense, venue, kickoff window and forecast. Teammates share the game's pass volume, so paired legs are priced together. A leg makes the list only when the model's number beats the price by a clear margin, the player's own record supports it, and a plain-language reason survives. Games a teammate missed count at 30%, games under a different quarterback at 50%, deep rungs are excluded, and players on a new team are shown but never picked.
